# How to Complete Texas Form VTR‑271

## What is Form VTR‑271?
Texas Form VTR‑271 is the **Power of Attorney (POA)** form used by the Texas Department of Motor Vehicles. It allows a person (the “grantor”) to authorize another person (the “grantee”) to sign certain vehicle documents on their behalf.

This form is commonly used when:
- A title runner is handling paperwork for a customer
- The seller or buyer cannot appear in person
- Someone needs authority to sign the title, registration application, or other TXDMV documents

## Information you will need
To complete VTR‑271 correctly, you must have:

- **Vehicle details:** VIN, year, make, model
- **Grantor information:** full legal name, address, and driver license/ID number
- **Grantee information:** full legal name and address
- **Purpose of the POA:** what the grantee is authorized to sign (title, registration, odometer disclosure, etc.)
- **Signatures:** grantor signature is required; grantee signature is optional but recommended
- **Notary:** VTR‑271 **must be notarized** for TXDMV to accept it

## Grantor (Person Giving Authority)
The grantor must provide:

- Full legal name
- Physical address
- Driver license or government ID number
- Signature (must be ink)
- Date of signing
- Acknowledgment before a notary public

The grantor is the person who owns the vehicle or has legal authority over it.

## Grantee (Person Receiving Authority)
The grantee is the person who will act on behalf of the grantor. They must provide:

- Full legal name
- Address
- Contact information
- Signature (optional but recommended)

The grantee is typically:
- A title runner
- A dealership representative
- A family member
- Anyone authorized to sign TXDMV documents for the grantor

## Vehicle information
This section identifies the vehicle for which the POA applies:

- VIN
- Year
- Make
- Model

TXDMV requires this information to ensure the POA is tied to a specific vehicle.

## Notary requirement
Form VTR‑271 **must be notarized**.
The notary will:

- Verify the grantor’s identity
- Witness the grantor’s signature
- Complete the notary section (seal, date, commission details)

Without notarization, the county tax office will reject the form.
